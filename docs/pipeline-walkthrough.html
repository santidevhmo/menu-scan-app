The thirteen operations

One dish's journey. The third lane is our hardcoded gram table — notice it stays idle until step 11.

Is the size calculated first?
No — last. Ingredients and their composition come first. Size is a single multiplication applied at the very end, to a list that already exists.
Is each ingredient separated?
Yes, by the model. It returns a structured list. Our code never parses prose — it consumes that array and does arithmetic on it.
Where does the hardcoded table fit?
Step 11 of 13. After both model passes and after the label call. It is the only place a number we typed by hand enters a dish.
How many times do we do the maths?
Twice. Once when the ingredients arrive, once more after rescaling. The model is never asked to add.

The one split that decides everything. Step 1 is a regex over the menu text, and it runs before any model sees the dish. If it finds a printed weight, the dish skips pass 2 and skips the table entirely — the page already said what it weighs. If it finds nothing, both stages apply. Every difference between the three dishes below traces back to that one field.

The middle column is the uncomfortable one. Pass 2 exists because it raises the score across 57 dishes — but on this draw it took a correct Salmón Roll and made it wrong, cutting the rice from 150 g to 60 g. Sizing then recovered most of the damage. Every stage here is an average-case improvement, not a per-dish guarantee, and that is the same mechanism behind a diner re-scanning one menu and getting different macros.

And the model is good at exactly what it is asked for. Across all three dishes the per-100 g compositions are sound, the ingredient lists are plausible, and protein — the number that follows most directly from naming the right foods — lands within 0.4% on the dish we can grade exactly. What goes wrong is how much arrives, and what the menu never said. Those are the two things the last three steps exist to attack.

Reproduce it. The first command costs ~$0.12 and rewrites the trace; the rest are $0.

deno run --allow-read --allow-write --allow-net --allow-env \
  --env-file=.env.local scripts/trace-dish.ts      # 9 calls, writes dish-traces.json
deno run --allow-read scripts/score-traces.ts      # scores it with the harness's own scorer
